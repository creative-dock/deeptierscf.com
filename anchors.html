The exposure

A stopped line costs millions a day. The cause is usually two tiers down.

Deep-tier failures rarely announce themselves. A small firm runs out of working capital, defers a material purchase, misses a delivery to your tier 1, and you find out when the schedule slips. This is exposure you already carry; nothing here adds any to your balance sheet.

No direct relationship, no line of sight

You do buy from the deeper tiers; their work is priced into the invoice your tier 1 sends you. You just hold no contract with them, so nothing about them is documented on your side.

Financial stress reaches you as a stoppage

The firm that cannot fund next month's material is the firm that stalls your manufacturing, delays your shipments, and misses your own customer's delivery date.

There is now a way to fund them

Your deeper tiers can reach financing at better terms without building out a full credit line of their own, and without you standing behind it.

What you get

Visibility as the return on data you already share

  • Resilience, not just insight. Suppliers that get funded keep producing, which is the cheapest disruption mitigation available to you.
  • Early warning on firms you never invoice. Rising funding demand, slipping payments, stretched cycles: the smoke before the fire.
  • A mapped deeper supply base. Which firms sit behind each tier 1, and where an operation is sole sourced or running on a single site.
Your obligations
  • Permissioned ERP access to the purchase orders and contracts you already issue to your tier 1s. Read-only, governed by a data agreement.
  • Nothing else. No facility to contract, no guarantee to give, no confirmation of payables, no change to your payment terms.
