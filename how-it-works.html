Why the link matters

Funders have no access to operational data, so they cannot see that an investment grade company asked for this production. Without that, a tier 2 invoice is only a claim on a small company's word, priced accordingly.

The link exists to build a trusted source of information, so the bank has certainty that the operation originates with a well rated company. Same invoice, entirely different risk.

FAQ

The questions we get first

What this is

Is this a supply chain finance programme?
No. There is nothing to enrol in and nothing to run inside treasury. We finance one transaction at a time, and we repeat it for every invoice a supplier wants funded.
Is this factoring, or a sale of my receivables?
No. Your invoice is not bought and sold on. It stays a normal invoice between you and your customer, who still pays it on the original due date. What changes is that a funder will advance against it, because the transaction behind it has been verified.
What does the anchor or tier 1 actually have to do?
Grant read-only access to trade documents they already exchange. That is the whole ask: no facility, no guarantee, no payables confirmation. A tier 2 supplier applies on its own initiative, but verification depends on the tiers above having granted that access first.
Does anyone's payment terms change?
No. Tier 1 pays the invoice on its original due date, and the anchor's terms with tier 1 are untouched. The financing sits between tier 2 and the funder.

Money and risk

Why would a tier 2 supplier get a better rate?
Because the funder is not pricing a small company in isolation. It can see that the demand originates with an investment grade anchor and that a tier 1 received the goods, so the rate can reflect the chain's credit quality instead of the supplier's size.
What does it cost, and who pays?
The funder prices each transaction individually, as a discount against the invoice being financed. You see the full terms before you accept anything, and you are free to decline and wait for your customer to pay as normal. There is no joining fee and no charge for the anchor or tier 1.
Who takes the credit risk, and what if my customer doesn't pay?
The funder takes the credit position; we are the verification layer that makes its assessment possible, and we do not lend. Whether an advance carries recourse back to the supplier is set in the funder's terms for that transaction, so read them before you accept. What we can say plainly is that no personal guarantee or collateral is asked for.

Data and practicalities

What data do you need, and who else can see it?
The order or contract from the anchor to tier 1, the order from tier 1 to tier 2, and enough master data to identify parties and parts. From tier 2, the invoice and proof of delivery. Access is read-only, scoped by agreement, and nobody is asked for their general ledger. Commercial detail is not passed between suppliers; each party sees its own transactions and the analysis produced for it.
How much work is the connection for our IT team?
Less than a treasury onboarding, which is the point. We read documents that already leave your system, so the connection is a scoped, read-only feed rather than a change to how you work. We size the effort against your own systems before anyone commits.
How quickly can a transaction be funded?
Verification runs on documents that already exist, so a decision does not wait on a new credit file being built from scratch. Once the tiers above have granted access, the pace is set by the funder's own review rather than by paperwork we are still chasing.
What happens if the documents don't reconcile?
The application stops. Quantities, parties, dates and values have to agree across the chain. A mismatch gets surfaced, not smoothed over; that is precisely what makes the record worth anything to a funder.
